# About GAPS

GAPS (Guidance for Artifact Preparation and Sharing) is an Open Educational Resource designed to support researchers in preparing, documenting, validating, publishing, preserving, and reusing research artifacts, particularly in Empirical Software Engineering.

## Purpose and scope

GAPS (Guidance for Artifact Preparation and Sharing) is an Open Educational Resource designed to support researchers in preparing, documenting, validating, publishing, preserving, and reusing research artifacts, particularly in Empirical Software Engineering research.

GAPS provides practical guidance to help researchers identify and address relevant aspects of artifact preparation and sharing throughout the research lifecycle. It is intended to support informed decisions and progressive improvement, rather than prescribe a single workflow or establish a universal standard for artifact quality.

The guidance is not intended to be exhaustive, and not all recommendations are essential or applicable to every research artifact. Their relevance depends on the artifact's purpose, characteristics, and intended use, as well as the level of coverage and transparency researchers aim to provide. Practical considerations, such as available time, funding, personnel, technical resources, and ethical or legal restrictions, may also influence which recommendations can be implemented.

Researchers are therefore encouraged to use GAPS selectively, identifying and prioritizing recommendations that are relevant to their context and feasible to address. Some recommendations may be essential for a particular artifact, while others may be optional, unnecessary, or disproportionate to its purpose. Addressing relevant recommendations incrementally can still contribute to improving an artifact, even when it is not feasible or appropriate to implement every recommendation.

GAPS is a reference to support researchers' judgment, not a compliance checklist that every artifact must satisfy in full. Its purpose is to help researchers make artifact preparation more deliberate and transparent, while recognizing that appropriate practices vary across research contexts.

## GAPS components

GAPS consists of two main components: **GAPS Material** and **GAPS Advisor**.

For a visual overview of GAPS and its components, explore the [GAPS Mind Map](/resources/GAPS-mind-map.md), which provides an interactive view of the resource's structure and main topics. For descriptions of the modules and complementary resources, visit the [GAPS home page](/README.md), which presents an overview of their content and purpose.

### GAPS Material

GAPS Material is the core educational resource. It is organized into ten topic-based modules covering different aspects of the research artifact lifecycle. The modules can be followed in sequence or consulted independently according to the researcher's needs.

The material also includes complementary resources to support its practical application:

- **Researcher-facing Self-check:** a checklist for reviewing an artifact and identifying aspects that may require attention before sharing.
- **README Template:** a reusable template for documenting an artifact and providing information and instructions for its use.
- **GAPS References:** a collection of sources supporting the material and providing opportunities for further reading.

For a detailed overview, see the [GAPS structure](/resources/structure.md). The complete content is also available in [GAPS all-in-one](/content/GAPS-all-in-one.md).

### GAPS Advisor

GAPS Advisor is a conversational assistant that helps users find and understand recommendations from GAPS Material in the context of their questions and research needs.

The Advisor supports navigation through the guidance but does not execute research artifacts or automatically inspect repositories. Its responses should be considered guidance to support researchers' decisions, not a substitute for examining the artifact and its specific requirements.

Access the [GAPS Advisor](https://notebook.google.com/notebook/17518b1f-e320-48be-a9c2-0edee13c6737).

## Development and sources

The content of GAPS was developed based on a synthesis of needs and practices identified through survey responses, complemented by relevant research literature, guidelines, standards, and materials suggested by survey participants.

These sources informed the practical guidance presented in the resource. The recommendations are intended to help researchers make informed decisions when preparing and sharing artifacts, while recognizing that appropriate practices may depend on the characteristics and constraints of each study.

The [GAPS References](/resources/references.md) page provides the sources supporting the material for consultation and further reading. Every effort has been made to acknowledge the sources used. If an attribution has been omitted unintentionally, feedback is welcome so that the necessary correction can be made.
